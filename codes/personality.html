My Hero: Dr. Mae Jemison

Why she inspires me: Dr. Jemison is not only an astronaut but also a physician, engineer, and passionate advocate for STEM equity. She embodies multidisciplinary excellence and fearlessness. I rewatch her TED talks whenever I doubt my path — her vision of inclusive science fuels my mission to bring tech education to underserved Jamaican communities.

AI Business Report: "AI-Powered Personalized Tutoring for Underserved Regions"

Favourite idea from ClickUp's AI Businesses 2025: An adaptive AI tutor that provides real-time homework help and curriculum support in low-bandwidth areas.

Application in my world (Jamaica): Many rural students lack access to extra tuition. This AI could be embedded into a mobile app that works offline, offering explanations in local dialect + standard English. It would assist with math, literacy, and basic coding — reducing educational inequality.

Why it's useful: Personalized learning at scale, 24/7 availability, reduces dependency on expensive tutors, and helps teachers by identifying student gaps.

Ethical Issues & Solutions: Bias in training data could marginalize local accents or reinforce stereotypes. Solution: Co-create datasets with local educators, ensure transparent algorithms, and build in human oversight. Privacy concerns: student data must be encrypted and never sold. Solution: Open-source auditing and parental consent dashboards.

Real app example: Khanmigo by Khan Academy — AI tutor using GPT but tailored for guided learning. Their ethical framework includes safeguards and teacher monitoring.
